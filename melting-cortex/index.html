A slice of
cortex, melting

Pyramidal neurons, the main cells of the cortex: triangle-shaped bodies, one long apical dendrite reaching up, spines on every branch. Watch for cells firing. Move your cursor over them, then scroll down.

How it's built

Three layers, each one simple

This page borrows the technique behind the butterfly-wing background on metamorphic.com, with different footage. Nothing here is a prerendered video: the "footage" is drawn live, so you can turn each layer on and off.

Layer 1

The footage

A hidden canvas grows 26 pyramidal neurons once (cell body, apical dendrite and tuft, basal dendrites, spines, axon with boutons) over a felt of faint fibres. Every frame it pans across them like a microscope stage, and now and then a cell fires: its body flashes and a pulse runs down the axon. In the original site this layer is a real MP4 of a wing.

drawImage(tissue) + action-potential pulses

Layer 2

The shader

Each frame is uploaded to the GPU. A fragment shader false-colours it by brightness, finds edges by comparing neighbouring pixels, and lower down the page bends the image with two sine waves and those edges, then dissolves it into the page colour.

texture(uSrc, uv + warp)

Layer 3

The contours

A transparent canvas on top shrinks what's on screen to a 128 × 80 grid, treats brightness as height, and traces topographic lines with marching squares. Only the lines near your cursor are drawn. On a phone, a point drifts around by itself.

marchingSquares(grid, level)

Tip: "1 · Footage" shows the raw drawing, before the shader colours it.
